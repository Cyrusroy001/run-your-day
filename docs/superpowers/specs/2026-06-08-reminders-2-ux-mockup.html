Why this feels like “good hands,” not a hustle tracker

Anchor — your real, fixed boundaries (from your JSON) On track / done Trimmed / attention Dropped / past (never red)
Calm by default, intelligence on demand. The home is quiet until the day actually drifts — then one plain sentence explains what the engine did and why. The engine is visible as competence, not numbers. No dashboards, no scores, no streaks — just “I trimmed Brunch to hold Work,” which reads as a capable assistant, not a taskmaster. It treats a day as human. Rest blocks are protected, not “optimized”; lateness is absorbed, not punished; nothing is ever deleted forever (only “removed for today”). Red is banned — amber is the loudest the app gets. It’s lifestyle-agnostic. Frames 3, 4 and 8 are the *same components* — a nurse, a parent, a student each see their own anchors and items because the UI renders the Life JSON, never Cyrus.
